# Relevant Brand System

Date: March 6, 2026
Scope: Brand language, personality, visual direction, copy principles, and section architecture for all Relevant touchpoints. This is the source of truth for any AI agent writing copy, designing UI, or making creative decisions for Relevant.

## Why this exists

Every AI agent working on Relevant needs a shared understanding of who this brand is, how it talks, and what it will never say. This document prevents drift into generic AI/news/productivity language.

- show the product promise immediately
- prove the mechanism visually
- reveal the surfaces that make the promise real
- explain the logic only after the user wants it
- end with access and path selection

This document is the source of truth for future design and copy decisions.

## Product Truth

Relevant is not a generic news app.

Relevant is a relevance engine that turns ambient information into ranked signal by answering three things:

1. What changed
2. Why it matters to you
3. What to do next

That is the product. Everything on the homepage should reinforce that.

## What flagship product pages do well

Observed pattern on Apple’s official iPhone product page on March 7, 2026:

- It opens with a clean product statement, not a paragraph.
- It quickly moves into a small set of memorable capability pillars.
- It proves each pillar with highly visual modules rather than dense explanation.
- It broadens into ecosystem, use context, and purchase/access paths after the core story is clear.
- It lets the product visuals do most of the persuasion.

Relevant should follow the same storytelling logic, but with a sharper, more editorial, more consequence-driven tone.

## Brand Persona

**Archetype: The Sharp Operator**

Relevant sounds like a strategist who respects your time. Not friendly-helpful, not corporate-safe. Direct, consequence-aware, slightly commanding. The brand assumes the product is good and shows rather than sells.

Relevant should sound like:

- a sharp operator who cuts to what matters
- a clear editor who kills filler on sight
- a system that respects ambition and time
- a product that cuts, ranks, and surfaces

Relevant should not sound like:

- a cheerful productivity app
- a generic AI assistant
- a lifestyle wellness brand
- a vague "stay ahead" SaaS company
- any product that uses "empower" or "discover"

## Brand Values

- Clarity over volume
- Consequence over curiosity
- Action over passive awareness
- Discipline over doom scrolling
- Relevance over recency
- Directness over polish-for-polish’s-sake

## Voice Rules

- Use short declarative sentences.
- Prefer blunt language over padded language.
- Lead with the consequence, not the category.
- Write with conviction.
- Make the user feel sharper, not entertained.
- Keep every section visually dominant and verbally compressed.

## Copy Rules

Use language like:

- Know what changed.
- See why it matters.
- Move before the window closes.
- Signal in. Clarity out.
- Ranked by consequence.
- Built for people who act.

Avoid language like:

- Stay ahead of the curve
- Personalized content for everyone
- Discover insights
- Empower your workflow
- Smarter news for modern professionals
- Never miss out again

## Core Messaging Rhythm

The entire brand should repeatedly return to this rhythm:

- what changed
- why it matters
- what to do next

This is the clearest expression of the product and should be treated as the main narrative spine, not just a sub-feature.

## Visual Direction

The website should feel like editorial intelligence meets live systems.

Direction:

- dark or near-dark base with controlled light surfaces
- live data streams and signal-routing motion
- modular visual cards instead of long text blocks
- product-native UI components, not decorative mockups
- asymmetry and layered depth, not flat marketing grids
- premium motion with purpose, not constant animation everywhere

Visual motifs to use:

- flowing source inputs
- ranked outputs
- signal clusters
- consequence badges
- finite stacks instead of infinite feeds
- branching paths for consumer vs team use

Visual motifs to avoid:

- generic SaaS dashboards
- empty glassmorphism for its own sake
- giant paragraphs beside screenshots
- static phone frames as the primary proof
- abstract gradients with no relationship to the product

## Recommended Landing Page Structure

This should be the section order for the main homepage.

### 1. Hero: The Product In One Sentence

Purpose:
- establish the product in one hit
- show the mechanism in motion
- make the user understand that this is a system, not a feed

Visual:
- animated source streams moving from multiple inputs into a relevance engine
- ranked signal cards exiting the engine
- one live product surface layered into the hero, not just a static screenshot

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Built for signal, not scrolling
- Headline: Know what changed before it changes your week.
- Body: Relevant turns ambient information into ranked signal for your work, goals, and next move.
- Supporting line: What changed. Why it matters. What to do next.
- CTA 1: Request early access
- CTA 2: See how it works

Why this comes first:
- the hero should introduce both the product and the product logic
- the motion itself should already explain the company

### 2. Signature Proof: Signal In, Clarity Out

Purpose:
- prove the engine visually before listing features
- show the input-to-output transformation in a way users can understand without reading much

Visual:
- animated pipeline with source labels, scoring nodes, ranking motion, and final action cards
- three to five compact proof cards with different consequence types

Draft copy:

- Kicker: The engine
- Headline: Noise goes in. Decision-grade signal comes out.
- Body: Relevant does not summarize the world. It ranks what has consequence for you.

Micro labels:

- Sources
- Lens
- Consequence
- Rank
- Next move

Why this is second:
- this is the equivalent of Apple’s early capability reveal
- it gives the user a memorable mental model before they see modules and screens

### 3. Product Surfaces: What the Product Actually Feels Like

Purpose:
- show the core consumer experience through a small number of living modules
- turn screenshots into interactive, website-native product scenes whenever possible

Visual:
- four large visual cards or modules
- each module should be partially usable or animated, not just described

Recommended modules:

1. Lens setup
2. Ranked feed
3. Signal detail
4. Goal/coach or Ask AI follow-up

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Inside the product
- Headline: Four surfaces. One job.
- Body: Every surface exists to shorten the distance between change and action.

Card copy:

- Lens setup: Tell the engine what should interrupt your day.
- Ranked feed: Open to the signals already worth your attention.
- Signal detail: Get the full picture without losing the thread.
- Goal coach / Ask AI: Push the signal into a decision, not another tab.

Why this is third:
- after users understand the model, they want to see the product
- this section should replace most of the existing descriptive feature text

### 4. How It Works: The Logic, Not the Marketing

Purpose:
- explain the operating sequence in the clearest possible way
- keep it mechanical and visual

Visual:
- a horizontal or vertical flow with four clear steps
- moving connectors, score shifts, lens toggles, and output states

Recommended sequence:

1. Set your lens
2. Watch for consequence
3. Rank the window
4. Act with context

Draft copy:

- Kicker: How it works
- Headline: Set the lens. Let the system watch.
- Body: Relevant filters weak matches, scores consequence, and surfaces the move while it still matters.

Why this is fourth:
- users now have enough context to care how the engine works
- this keeps explanation after product proof, not before it

### 5. Why Relevant Exists: The Philosophy Section

Purpose:
- define the worldview behind the product
- make the brand feel intentional, not merely functional

Visual:
- manifesto-style statement cards
- contrast between infinite noise and finite signal
- hard-edged editorial layouts

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Why it exists
- Headline: You do not need more information. You need the right interruption.
- Body: Relevant exists because high-agency people should not have to dig through noise to find what already matters.

Possible philosophy cards:

- Finite feed
- Consequence first
- No guilt loops
- Built for people who move

Why this belongs here:
- philosophy lands better after the user already understands the system
- this section creates emotional clarity without becoming founder-diary copy

### 6. Path Split: Personal Edge or Team Edge

Purpose:
- keep the homepage product-agnostic while acknowledging two clear use cases
- prepare the future B2B page without forcing a full branch now

Visual:
- two bold path cards
- one consumer-facing, one team-facing
- each card should feel like a doorway, not a footer link

Recommended framing:

- For your edge
- For your team

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Choose your path
- Headline: Same engine. Different stakes.
- Body: Use Relevant for your own operating edge now, or bring the same logic into a team workflow later.

Card copy:

- For your edge: Sharper daily signal for work, learning, and next moves.
- For your team: Role-aware intelligence for people who need to act on change together.

Why this is sixth:
- it keeps the homepage broad without muddying the hero
- it creates a clean future branch into a dedicated B2B page

### 7. Access Section: Pricing, Waitlist, and Entry

Purpose:
- close with simple access language
- make the action obvious

Visual:
- one strong waitlist card
- simple pricing tile
- no clutter

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Get access
- Headline: Get in before the public launch.
- Body: Early access gets the sharpest version first.
- Pricing line: Free 7-day trial at launch. Then $4.99/month.
- CTA: Request early access

Why this closes the page:
- by the time the user reaches this point, they should already understand what the product is and why it matters

## What to remove or compress from the current site

- Long explanatory paragraphs in the hero
- Separate sections that repeat the same “role-aware relevance engine” message in different words
- Generic feature-chip sections without strong visual proof
- Audience lists that say who it is for before the product has fully landed

## What to keep from the current site

- The three-part product model
- The product-led direction of the current `src/app/page.tsx`
- The stronger “role-aware relevance engine” positioning
- The ranking / consequence framing
- The idea of Signal Forge and ranked outputs

## Implementation Guidance

When redesigning the homepage, build toward these priorities:

1. Replace static hero staging with a true signal-flow animation.
2. Consolidate “features” and “process” into fewer, more visual modules.
3. Recreate product surfaces as live website components wherever practical.
4. Keep text minimal and let motion plus layout carry explanation.
5. Introduce the consumer/team split low on the page, not in the hero.

## Copy Reference Lines

These are directional lines that fit the brand:

- The world moves. Relevant ranks the part that matters.
- See the shift before it hits your work.
- Ranked for consequence, not clicks.
- Built for people who act before the summary gets stale.
- The feed ends. The signal stays.
- Less reading. Better timing.

## One-line Brand Test

If a line sounds like it could belong to any AI, productivity, or news product, it is wrong for Relevant.

If a line sounds sharper, more decisive, and more consequence-aware than the category norm, it is probably right.

**Quick test:** Read the line aloud. If you could paste it onto Notion's homepage, Feedly's landing page, or any "AI news summarizer" without it feeling out of place — rewrite it. Relevant copy should make you feel slightly uncomfortable with how direct it is. That's the signal it's working.
