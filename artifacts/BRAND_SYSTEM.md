# Relevant - Homepage Brand System

Date: March 7, 2026
Scope: Main homepage story, brand language, section order, and visual rules for Relevant.

This file is the homepage source of truth.
Any future AI agent or designer touching the website should use this before changing copy, layout, or visual direction.

## What Relevant Is

Relevant is not a news app.
Relevant is not a feed aggregator.
Relevant is not a summary tool.

Relevant is a relevance engine.
It watches ambient information, filters it through a user's lens, scores consequence, and outputs three things:

1. What happened
2. Why it matters to you
3. What to do next

That is the product.
The homepage should explain that system before it explains any individual feature.

## What We Learned From Apple Product Pages

After reviewing Apple's official iPhone product-page structure on March 7, 2026, the pattern is clear:

- start with one clean product statement
- move quickly into a small number of memorable capability pillars
- prove those pillars with visual modules, not paragraphs
- explain the deeper mechanics only after the user wants them
- end with ecosystem, comparison, and access paths

Relevant should follow that structure, but with a harder edge.
Apple sells aspiration through polish.
Relevant should sell consequence through clarity.

## Brand Persona

Relevant should feel like:

- a sharp operator
- a brutally clear editor
- a system that respects time
- a product built for people who act

Relevant should not feel like:

- a cheerful productivity app
- a generic AI tool
- a wellness brand for information overload
- a vague "stay ahead" SaaS company

## Brand Values

- Clarity over volume
- Consequence over curiosity
- Action over passive awareness
- Discipline over dopamine
- Relevance over recency
- Directness over politeness theater
- Respect for time over engagement tricks

## Voice Rules

- Use short declarative sentences.
- Be blunt, not rude.
- Lead with the consequence.
- Avoid padded marketing language.
- Make the user feel sharper, not comforted.
- If a sentence sounds generic, cut it.

## Copy Rules

Use language like:

- Know what changed.
- See why it matters.
- Move before the window closes.
- Noise in. Signal out.
- Ranked by consequence.
- Built for people who act.
- Information you should read.

Avoid language like:

- Stay ahead of the curve
- Discover insights
- Empower your workflow
- Personalized content for everyone
- AI-powered intelligence for modern professionals
- Never miss out again

## Homepage Principle

The homepage is not a brochure.
It is a product experience.

Every section should answer one of these questions:

1. What is this?
2. What does it do?
3. How does it work?
4. Why does it exist?
5. Which version is for me?
6. How do I get access?

If a section does not answer one of those questions, remove it.

## Visual Rules

- Text-light, visual-heavy
- Dark or near-dark base with controlled light surfaces
- Motion used to explain the system, not decorate the page
- Product-native UI over static screenshots whenever possible
- Strong hierarchy: one headline, one line of support, one visual idea
- No big essay blocks beside screenshots

Visual motifs to use:

- source streams
- ranking nodes
- signal cards
- consequence chains
- finite stacks
- decision windows
- path split cards for individual vs team

Visual motifs to avoid:

- fake SaaS dashboards
- glassmorphism with no meaning
- decorative gradients that do not map to the product
- endless icon grids
- static phone mockups as primary proof

## Homepage Story Order

This is the correct section order for the main landing page.

### 1. Hero

Purpose:
- introduce the product in one sentence
- show the relevance engine in motion
- make the user understand this is a system, not another feed

Visual:
- many incoming headline or source streams
- those streams converge into a relevance engine
- a few ranked signal cards emerge on the other side
- one live product surface layered into the hero

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Built for signal, not scrolling
- Headline: Know what changed before it changes your week.
- Body: Relevant turns ambient information into ranked signal for your work, decisions, and next move.
- Support line: What happened. Why it matters to you. What to do next.
- CTA 1: Request early access
- CTA 2: See how it works

### 2. Engine Proof

Purpose:
- prove the transformation before listing features
- give the user a fast mental model for the product

Visual:
- source labels flowing into scoring nodes
- the lens filtering weak matches
- ranked cards exiting the system
- micro-labels for each stage

Draft copy:

- Kicker: The engine
- Headline: Noise goes in. Decision-grade signal comes out.
- Body: Relevant does not summarize the world. It filters for consequence.

Micro labels:

- Sources
- Lens
- Consequence
- Rank
- Next move

### 3. Product Surfaces

Purpose:
- show what the product actually feels like
- replace descriptive paragraphs with live, visual modules

Visual:
- four large interactive or semi-interactive cards
- each card should behave like a real product surface

Recommended modules:

1. Lens setup
2. Ranked feed
3. Signal detail
4. Goal coach or Ask AI follow-up

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Inside the product
- Headline: Four surfaces. One job.
- Body: Every screen exists to shorten the distance between change and action.

Card copy:

- Lens setup: Tell the engine what should interrupt your day.
- Ranked feed: Open to the few signals already worth your attention.
- Signal detail: Get the full picture without losing the thread.
- Goal coach / Ask AI: Push the signal into a decision, not another tab.

### 4. How It Works

Purpose:
- explain the operating logic in the clearest possible way
- keep it mechanical, not promotional

Visual:
- a four-step flow with moving connectors and rank shifts
- each step should feel like part of one system

Sequence:

1. Set your lens
2. Watch for consequence
3. Rank the window
4. Act with context

Draft copy:

- Kicker: How it works
- Headline: Set the lens. Let the system watch.
- Body: Relevant filters weak matches, scores consequence, and surfaces the move while it still matters.

### 5. Why Relevant Exists

Purpose:
- explain the philosophy behind the product
- make the brand feel opinionated, not generic

Visual:
- three principle cards
- one manifesto line
- finite-feed and anti-noise cues

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Why this exists
- Headline: Most information makes you feel busy. Relevant is built to make you sharper.
- Body: No infinite feed. No guilt loop. No passive awareness. Just the few things you should know while they still matter.

Principle cards:

- Finite by default
- Consequence over curiosity
- Built for people who act

### 6. Path Split

Purpose:
- keep the homepage product-agnostic
- acknowledge both the consumer app and the team product
- prevent the homepage from collapsing into either a B2C page or a B2B page

Rule:
- the split appears only after the core engine is understood
- do not lead the homepage with "consumer vs business"

Visual:
- two large branch cards
- left card uses consumer-app surfaces
- right card uses command-center surfaces

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Two ways in
- Headline: Use it yourself. Or run it across a team.
- Body: Same engine. Different surface area.

Card copy:

- Individual app: Your personal signal stack for work, learning, goals, and daily sharpness.
- Team command center: Shared lenses, decision queues, role exposure, and live context for organizations.

CTA labels:

- Explore the app
- Explore team mode

Future rule:
- later, the team card can link to a dedicated B2B page
- the main homepage should stay engine-first

### 7. Access

Purpose:
- close the page with one clean path to action
- keep pricing and waitlist clear, not salesy

Visual:
- one strong access card
- pricing tile
- minimal form

Draft copy:

- Kicker: Get access
- Headline: Get in before the public launch.
- Body: Join early for launch access, product drops, and the first sharp version of the system.
- Pricing note: Free 7-day trial at launch. Then $4.99/month.
- CTA: Request early access

## Section Copy Budget

Each section should aim for:

- 1 kicker
- 1 headline
- 1 short support paragraph
- 3 to 5 micro labels or card captions

If a section needs more than that, the visual is not working hard enough.

## Reuse Real Product Surfaces

Do not default to screenshots.
The real product surfaces already exist in the current workspace.

Consumer-app surfaces available to adapt into web components:

- onboarding intro and lens setup
- signal card
- signal detail tabs
- consequence chain
- signal-forge or loader visuals
- goal and coaching surfaces

Team surfaces available to adapt into web components:

- top moves grid
- decision queue
- role exposure grid
- live context ribbon

Rule:
- recreate these as web-native components
- use screenshots only when a direct recreation is too expensive for the current pass

## Final Direction

The homepage should leave the visitor with one thought:

This is not another place to read more.
This is the system that tells me what actually matters, why it matters now, and what I should do next.
